# Visual Reference Roles

Use this file with the user-supplied images retained in `visual-references/`. The set establishes two different visual roles: an overall document atmosphere and a vivid banner treatment. They belong in one system without becoming competing themes.

## Current interpretation

The governing art direction uses the user's “Reverend Mother” and “Bene Gesserit scroll” references as private shorthand. The document should feel like a carefully kept instrument of knowledge: composed, exact, self-possessed, and consequential. The reference concerns visual presence and the handling of knowledge, not a required prose voice or palette. Public documents stay direct and grounded; they must not name the fictional source, quote it, or reproduce its characters, language, costume, symbols, or plot.

For the design pass, imagine that a Reverend Mother arranged the page for someone who must learn from it later: discernment, self-command, memory, and mastery are visible in what is emphasized, what is held back until needed, and how carefully the reader is guided. The composition should carry this presence before the words do. It is a private art-direction test, never a public narrative or persona.

The images suggest composed figures, veiling and partial reveal, precise gestures, solemn portraiture, close framing, strong tonal contrast, and richly worked materials: layered drapery, patterned veils, beads, and worked surfaces. Translate these into editorial choices: hierarchy that feels deliberate, confident alignment, carefully paced sections, meaningful captions and cross-references, and image-and-text relationships with room to breathe. A poised hand, a measured silhouette, or one sharply framed material detail can carry more presence than a decorative emblem. Let the document carry authority through knowledge, precision, and controlled disclosure. Keep essential facts and actions easy to find; mystery is not a reason to obscure content.

The “scroll” is a reference to a consequential record that is preserved and consulted. It does not mean literal parchment, faux calligraphy, invented seals, ancient-looking effects, gold ornament, or a fixed dark palette. Avoid Gothic, occult, Greek-temple, horror, and fantasy shorthand. These exclusions guard the reference from becoming costume design.

The coral photographs show how a banner or another selected image moment can carry vivid natural color. One image is a blurred organic color field with coral-like forms along its lower edge; the other is a shallow soft-focus color band, not a detailed coral macro. They are image-role examples, not the source of the document's overall identity or body palette. A saturated banner can bring living color into a measured page. In a grayscale rendering, heading, scope, status, and evidence hierarchy must remain legible without hue as the only distinction.

The remaining files retain their supplied `.png` names and bytes; file inspection identifies JPEG encoding. Preserve them byte-for-byte. If a future renderer needs extension and MIME to agree, create a documented derivative for that target rather than silently rewriting the reference.

## Application rules

- Carry the entrusted-document atmosphere throughout the page through heading cadence, paragraph rhythm, image framing, sequence, and evidence handling. Restraint must feel deliberate and complete, not empty, generic, or unfinished.
- Make the document feel authored, kept, and useful: a reader should sense that its knowledge has been arranged with care and can be returned to.
- Use controlled disclosure to pace the reading and frame important material. Never hide facts, caveats, procedures, or evidence behind visual drama.
- Let the opening feel like a threshold, not a generic marketing hero. Establish identity and scope, then let a deliberate title treatment or approved image set the page's presence.
- Express presence through composition and handling: pre-cropped images, deliberate image-and-text relationships, precise captions, useful cross-references, section cadence, and restrained rules. A fixed color is not needed; use only typographic controls available in the target renderer.
- Keep the prose in the project's real voice. Do not make it sound like a fictional order, scripture, prophecy, or ceremonial proclamation.
- Use documentary details such as folio labels, dates, status, provenance, or marginal notes only when they carry real information; never invent an archival surface for atmosphere alone.
- Treat coral as a banner or selected image surface. Keep its vivid color local to that visual role unless the target codebase has an independently established palette.
- A banner may be assertive; the surrounding type, navigation, code, tables, and evidence can remain measured and task-focused.
- Do not make every image, heading, inline label, table, or code block repeat the banner treatment.
- Do not default to black-only styling, a fixed palette, a particular typeface, Gothic ornament, horror cues, occult decoration, faux antiquity, or ceremonial symbols.
- Do not publish these reference screenshots or their marks and text as project assets unless the user explicitly approves them for that use. When publishing an image, keep alt text, provenance, rights, and GitHub rendering in view.
- When future examples clarify or change an image's role, update this map while preserving each retained file byte-for-byte.
